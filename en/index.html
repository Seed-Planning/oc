Facing Ovarian Cancer
Insights from
Patients' Experiences

Diagnosis · Treatment · Daily Life · Emotional Support

Drawing on interviews with six patients who have experienced ovarian cancer diagnosis and treatment,
we explore the themes that emerged from their stories: the confusion of diagnosis, the impact

Meet the Patients

Patient A

She lives with her parents and works part-time two days a week. Although she left her previous job to undergo treatment, music and outings with friends have helped her cope. She hopes to return to a permanent, full-time role in the future.

Patient B

She lives with her husband and works mainly from home as a freelancer. She enjoys traveling and going to concerts, adjusting her workload to how she feels so that she can maintain a manageable pace.

Patient C

She lives with her husband and daughter. Currently on leave from work, she takes each day as her health allows. She does not want others to feel they have to be overly careful around her, and values her connections with patient communities and people on social media.

Patient D

She lives alone and works full-time at a drugstore. She has previously received hormone treatment for endometriosis and uterine fibroids, as well as medication for hip and lower back pain. She enjoys reading and cooking, and speaks openly with those around her about her illness and treatment.

Patient E

She lives with her husband and son and continues to work full-time. She has long enjoyed concerts and stage shows featuring her favorite pop performers. Her child was young when she was diagnosed, so she managed her hospital visits and treatment on her own. She has continued working by using paid leave, while also cherishing time with her family.

Patient F

She lives with her husband and two children and works full-time as an elementary school teacher. She enjoys traveling around Japan and sleeping in her car along the way. Continuing to work during treatment has been a great source of support, and seeing her students grow and graduate keeps her going.

Patient Stories

The Road to Diagnosis

Explore This Topic

Each patient's road to an ovarian cancer diagnosis began differently. Changes they noticed in everyday life led them to seek medical care: severe period pain, sudden abdominal swelling, a feeling of heaviness in the lower abdomen, bloating, leg pain or swelling, or an enlarged ovary identified during screening.

Treatment Choices After Diagnosis

Explore This Topic

Many patients felt deeply shocked and overwhelmed when they were told they had ovarian cancer. They described how difficult it was to take in such unexpected news: "My mind went blank," "It felt as though they were talking about someone else," and "I can barely remember what I was told."

How Treatment Affects Daily Life

Explore This Topic

Surgery, drug treatment, and radiotherapy place a considerable burden on the body. In these interviews, patients described a wide range of physical effects, including pain and reduced stamina after surgery, fatigue, numbness, hair loss, nausea, mouth sores, joint and muscle pain, infections, and difficulties with bowel and bladder function.

The Information Patients Needed

Explore This Topic

The interviews showed that patients used many sources to find information about treatment and daily life. These included explanations from doctors and nurses, public-sector websites, hospital materials, books, patient groups, social media, and blogs and videos shared by people with similar experiences.

Fear of Recurrence and Sources of Support

Explore This Topic

Worries do not necessarily disappear once a course of treatment is over. In these interviews, patients spoke about fear of recurrence, the tension of waiting for test results, concerns about tumor marker levels and scans, and uncertainty about future treatment choices.
